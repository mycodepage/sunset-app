The sunset score is a grade from 0 to 100 for how beautiful tonight's sunset is likely to be. It starts from five weather measures, then is reduced if the sky is grey, wet or blocked.

Labels: 0-24 Poor, 25-44 Fair, 45-64 Good, 65-81 Great, 82-100 Spectacular. The background colours also follow the score.

The reason line names the main thing shaping the grade. It shows the first problem found (storm, rain, fog, thick overcast, low cloud on the horizon, a clear sky, haze). If there is none, it describes a good sky.

Sunset time is in the local time of the place you chose. Best viewing starts 20 minutes earlier so you can be outside while the colours build. They often stay good for a while after the sun drops.

7-day outlook

Tap any day to see its score and measures. The number on each button is that day's sunset score. Days further ahead are less reliable, because cloud layers are hard to predict.

Background sky and clouds

The sky blends from grey-blue to deep blue, magenta and orange as the score rises, so you can read the day at a glance.

Drifting clouds appear when the afternoon sky is cloudy: more clouds mean more cover. Grey clouds mean a gloomy day, peach clouds mean colour is likely.

How the score works

The big picture

The app fetches an hourly forecast from Open-Meteo, measures five conditions around sunset, scores each from 0 to 1, combines them with weights into a base score, then multiplies it down if the sky is grey, wet or blocked. This is a heuristic built from how fire-sky sunsets form, not a scientific model.

The data and time windows

Hourly values: total, low, mid and high cloud cover, humidity, rain chance, rain amount, weather code and visibility.

  • Sunset window: the hour before, of and after sunset. Cloud layers, humidity, rain chance, visibility and weather code come from here.
  • Afternoon window: the 3 hours before sunset plus the sunset hour. Rain amount and total cloud come from here, to catch a wet, grey afternoon.

Visibility10%over 10 km0 at 2 km, 1 at 10 km
Humidity10%under 70%1 at 70%, 0 at 95%

Mid and high cloud overlap, so they are combined like probabilities: 100 x (1 - (1-mid) x (1-high)). 50% mid and 50% high gives 75%, not 100%.

base = 0.40 canopy + 0.25 low + 0.15 rain + 0.10 visibility + 0.10 humidity

The gates: why grey, wintery days score low

A weighted sum lets good values rescue a bad sky. Gates are multipliers from 0 to 1, and only the harshest one is used: score = base x lowest gate.

GateWhen it bites
Thick overcastMid + high cloud above 75%. Falls from 1.0 to 0.25 at 100%.
Blocked horizonLow cloud above 30%. Falls to 0.3 at 75%.
Wet weatherStorm 0.2, rain or snow 0.3, fog 0.35. Also rain over 0.2 mm in the afternoon (0.3 at 2 mm) and rain chance above 30% (0.4 at 70%).
Grey afternoonTotal cloud above 85% over the afternoon. Falls to 0.45 at 100%.
Worked examples

Fire sky: cloud 54%, low 25%, rain 12%, visibility 9 km, humidity 65%. Base 0.956, no gates. Score 96, Spectacular.

Grey winter evening: cloud 95%, everything else ideal. Base 0.62. The old formula gave 73. Gates: overcast 0.40, grey afternoon 0.63. Score 0.62 x 0.40 = 25, Fair.

Labels, reason line and gauges
  • Labels: 0-24 Poor, 25-44 Fair, 45-64 Good, 65-81 Great, 82-100 Spectacular.
  • Reason line: the first problem that applies, in this order: storm, rain or snow, fog, thick overcast, low cloud, clear sky, haze or damp air.
  • Gauges: colour comes from each measure's own sub-score, red at 0 through yellow to green at 1. Helping is 0.75 or more, Mixed is 0.40 to 0.75, Hurting is below 0.40. Gauges show a measure alone, before weights and gates.
Limits and tuning
  • Cloud layers are among the hardest things to forecast, so treat the score as a guide.
  • Clouds can clear or arrive within minutes of sunset.
  • Dust and aerosols, which can deepen reds, are not used yet.
  • To improve it, rate real sunsets for a week or two and adjust the ranges, weights and gate thresholds in the code when they disagree.
